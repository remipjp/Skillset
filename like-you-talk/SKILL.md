# PG Writing Principles

## Core rules

1. **Write like you talk.** Every sentence must pass: "Would I say this to a friend?" If not, replace it with what you'd actually say.
2. **Use ordinary words.** No fancy vocabulary to sound impressive. Plain words, simple sentences. Let ideas do the work.
3. **Cut everything unnecessary.** Write the first draft fast, then spend the real effort editing. Cutting simplifies naturally.
4. **Reduce friction to zero.** The less energy spent parsing prose, the more readers have left for ideas. If friction is low enough, they'll read to the end.
5. **Never be accidentally complex.** Use a complex sentence only when the idea demands it. Never because you weren't paying attention.

## Voice

- Conversational, not formal. Cleaned-up speech, not literary prose.
- No "penning" things. No "mercurial Spaniards." No phrases you'd never say aloud.
- Shorter sentences for simple ideas. Longer ones only when the thought is genuinely subtle.
- Match rhythm to the shape of the idea — not a uniform cadence.

## Process

1. **Draft fast** — get ideas down without polishing.
2. **Read aloud** — fix anything that doesn't sound like speech. Fix phonetically awkward passages too.
3. **Cut aggressively** — if a word isn't earning its place, remove it.
4. **Check for friction** — reread and ask: "Does anything catch? Does anything feel wrong?"
5. **Stuck? Say it out loud.** Explain the idea to someone (or imagine doing so), then write what you said.

## What to avoid

- **Em dashes (—)** — never use them. Use periods, commas, or restructure the sentence instead.
- Formal/literary word choices that distance the reader
- Complex sentences that give a false impression of depth
- Fancy prose used to mask thin ideas. If you say nothing simply, it's obvious
- Assuming difficult topics justify difficult language (many readers aren't native English speakers)

## Quality check

- [ ] Every sentence passes the "would I say this?" test
- [ ] Ideas are prominent, words are invisible
- [ ] No accidentally complex sentences
- [ ] Rhythm varies with the ideas, not uniform
- [ ] A non-native English speaker could follow this
