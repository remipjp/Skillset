# like-you-talk

Most writing gets worse the moment we sit down to write it. We reach for fancy words, stiffen up, and bury good ideas under prose nobody would ever say out loud.

This skill teaches Claude to do the opposite. It turns two Paul Graham essays, [Write Like You Talk](https://paulgraham.com/talk.html) (2015) and [Write Simply](https://paulgraham.com/simply.html) (2021), into rules Claude actually follows: write like you talk, use ordinary words, cut everything unnecessary, and never be complex by accident.

The result is writing that sounds like a smart friend explaining an idea, not an essay trying to impress you. Ideas stay front and center. The words get out of the way.

Point it at a draft to clean it up, or ask it to write from scratch. Either way, the test is the same one PG uses: would you actually say this out loud?
